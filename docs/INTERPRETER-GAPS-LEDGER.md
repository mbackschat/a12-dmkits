# interpreter — gaps & open-issues ledger

> **What this is** — the interpreter's peer of [`DEV-API-GAPS-LEDGER.md`](DEV-API-GAPS-LEDGER.md) and [`FEATURE-GAPS-LEDGER.md`](FEATURE-GAPS-LEDGER.md): the single tracking surface for **open `:interpreter` gaps, deferred items, and to-fix issues**. It exists so open work is easy to **track, report, and pick up** — and so [`INTERPRETER-FINDINGS.md`](INTERPRETER-FINDINGS.md) can be what a findings doc should be: **settled, verified knowhow** you read to warm up and not re-investigate (like `KERNEL-FINDINGS.md`), never a backlog of dispositions ("open / to fix / deferred"). Per the CLAUDE.md *"rationale is timeless; a disposition is status"* rule — the findings hold the timeless fact, this ledger holds the live status.
>
> **The lifecycle.** A gap is born here with an `IG<n>` code under the section matching its status (§1 open / §2 deferred / §3 out-of-scope). When it ships, three things happen **in the fix's own commit**: (1) its **rationale graduates to a numbered `IF<n>` finding** in [`INTERPRETER-FINDINGS.md`](INTERPRETER-FINDINGS.md) — that finding notes it "graduated from IGn"; (2) any code/test comment citing `IGn` is **repointed to `IFn`**; (3) the gap's row is **deleted from this ledger**. **There is no "fixed/shipped" section — we don't record fixed gaps.** A fixed gap is no longer a gap; **git is the history of *when/what* was fixed, and the `IFn` finding is the *why*** — a hand-kept "shipped" list would only duplicate both and rot. So this ledger only ever lists **live, unfinished work** (the already-shipped IF20-per-cell / IF21 / IF23 / IF24 / IF25 live as findings, not here). A **deferred** gap stays in §2 with its re-open trigger, and an **ongoing as-touched** refactor stays in §2 with its remaining-work note (it is progressing in increments, not parked — its shipped parts graduate to its `IF<n>` finding while the row stays until *all* of it lands); the technical detail of each gap lives in its cited `IF<n>` finding (the knowhow), this ledger holds only *what's left to do and why*.
>
> **Section invariant (the placement rule):** every section holds exactly one **live** status — **§1 = open, §2 = deferred / ongoing-incremental, §3 = out-of-scope**. A FULLY-shipped/graduated entry belongs in **none** of them: completing it *deletes* its row (see the lifecycle). So §1 reads "(none)" whenever nothing is open, and a fully-FIXED entry **anywhere** in this ledger is a bug in *this doc* — git already records the fix. (A partially-shipped *ongoing* refactor in §2 is not "fixed" — it stays until all of it lands.)
>
> **Scope.** `:interpreter` only — the kernel-free Kotlin evaluator (parser, evaluation semantics, model loader, the differential/perf harnesses' interpreter side). dmkit (rulekit/modelkit/edit) authoring gaps go to [`DEV-API-GAPS-LEDGER.md`](DEV-API-GAPS-LEDGER.md); kernel-coverage classification stays in the MVK / RT-semantics / grammar ledgers.

## 1. Open (to fix)

A systematic **7-surface gap audit (2026-06-26, kernel-source-verified)** found the interpreter **materially short of full kernel parity beyond `validateFull`** — across the parser, `compute()`, the model loader, date construction, and partial validation (method: enumerate every surface where a divergence can hide — disposition prose, `unsupported`/throw census, entry-point × parameter dimensions, construct × entry-point matrix, loader-property diff, kernel ledgers cross-checked, differential-coverage holes — then verify each candidate against the kernel source). The bulk of the audit's confirmed divergences have since **shipped** (each graduated to a numbered `IF` finding + a differential lock and was **deleted** from here — git is the record). **Every audit-confirmed divergence across `validateFull` / `validatePart` / `compute` is closed** — but two open items below sit in blind spots the audit's *method* could not reach: **IG22** (a date-literal parse gap) and **IG23** (`$` outer-correlation, entirely unsupported). IG23 in particular exposes a limit of the audit itself — it enumerated constructs by the *operator inventory*, so a non-operator syntactic modifier like `$` was never in scope (see IG23's "why/how it hid"). partial validation matches the kernel (rule-gating + 3VL, subset aggregates IF35, per-operator starred-aggregate relevance IF42, global fields IF37, custom-condition relevant-set IF38, subset uniqueness IF39), and `compute()` is at parity (cascade, ERRORED bucket, errored-dependency skip). (Closed 2026-06-26: IG10→IF38, IG11→IF37, IG13→IF40, IG19→IF33 (parity-by-construction), the uniqueIndex/RepetitionNotUnique subset case→IF39, IG20→IF42.)

- **IG22 — the omitted-year date literal `"13.07."` (a day-month partial-date constant) is not modeled.** rulekit authors it (`DateLiteral`, `Dsl.dateValue` — KERNEL-FINDINGS §6 "with or without the year"), and the kernel stores it as a DATE constant, but the interpreter's `coerceDate` only canonicalizes the FULL `dd.MM.yyyy` form, so a `[SomeDate] > "13.07."` comparison isn't evaluated as a date (KERNEL-FINDINGS §6, "the omitted-year form is not yet modeled"). **Step 1 is a probe**, not code: confirm whether `[Date] op "13.07."` is even validation-legal and, if so, its exact runtime semantics (year injected? month/day only? no-fire?) — the clean-room reimplementation needs the kernel's behavior first. If it turns out validation-illegal, this closes as parity-by-construction (the interpreter never sees it). Surfaced by the 2026-07-06 findings sweep; not in the 2026-06-26 audit's three surfaces (validateFull/validatePart/compute).

- **IG23 — `$` outer-correlation is entirely unsupported by the interpreter** (parser + AST + evaluator), while dmkits reads/authors/renders it in full. A `$`-marked reference correlates a `Having` filter's inner row to the OUTER iteration row (`AtLeastOneFieldFilled(../G3*/F83 Having CurrentRepetition(/G1/G2/G3) != CurrentRepetition($/G1/G2/G3) And [F83] == [$F83])`, the kernel BA's "predecessors" pattern, KERNEL-FINDINGS §"$ correlation is implemented, both directions"). The interpreter hard-fails at **parse** (`parser: unexpected '$'`), so `model eval` / `rule eval` / `model compute` are **unusable on any model using it** — reported on a real corpus model (2026-07-06). The fix is a three-layer increment: **(1) parser** — accept `$` in all four atom positions (`[$path]`, `CurrentRepetition($path)`, bare presence `$path`, value-list `$Group*/field`) → an `outer` flag; **(2) AST** — add `outer: Boolean` to `Expr.Field` + the group-ref/value-list nodes; **(3) evaluator** — the correlation semantics, threading an OUTER `EvalContext` so a `$`-ref inside a `Having` resolves to the quantifier's own row, not the inner filtered row. Clean-room-reimplementable from KERNEL-FINDINGS §"$ correlation" + the BA "predecessors" example; lock with an interpreter differential (a `…DiffTest` that authors a `$`-correlated `Having` and runs BOTH engines — the very test whose absence hid this).

  **Why/how this huge gap hid (the process root cause, evidenced 2026-07-06).** The interpreter's parity is only as complete as the differential + fuzz *feed* it — and **`$` was never fed to `DmInterpreter`.** Every `$` test in the repo drives either the **kernel oracle** (`adapter.laws.CorrelationLawsTest` — 0 `DmInterpreter` refs) or the **adapter READ path** (`OuterCorrelation{Read,Quantifier,Authoring,Backstop}Test` — `KernelAdapter` convert), and the **fuzz never generates `$`** (`InterpreterKernelFuzzTest` emits no `$`/`Having`/`CurrentRepetition`). Three compounding blind spots let it pass unnoticed: **(a) the parity guards are OPERATOR-keyed** (`KernelConstructParityTest`, `CatalogParityTest` enumerate operator/construct *names*) but `$` is a non-operator syntactic *modifier* (an `outer` flag on a ref), so no net covers it; **(b) the grammar ledger classified `$` by the render/read lens** (`entitySpec` → "plumbing"; `$` → "read — kernel-delegated"), answering "does rulekit render/read it?" (yes) — never "does the interpreter parse+evaluate it?"; **(c) read-side thoroughness masked eval-side absence** — the dedicated `OuterCorrelation*` suite + the N8 `verifyOuterPreserved` backstop made `$` *look* done at the dmkits level, and the 2026-06-26 "7-surface audit" inherited the same operator-keyed construct set, so it declared "§1 empty" without ever probing `$` through the interpreter. **The durable prevention lever (part of the fix):** a guard keyed on the RENDERER's emit vocabulary — every construct `DslRenderer` can *emit* (which includes `$`) must round-trip through the interpreter parser — plus teaching the fuzz to generate `$`; then an "authored-but-never-interpreted" construct can't hide again. This is the class the operator-keyed guards structurally cannot see.

**A full renderer-vocabulary audit (2026-07-06) found five MORE gaps of the same class as IG23** — constructs `DslRenderer` emits that no interpreter differential ever fed to `DmInterpreter` (only `*ReadTest` render-checks or kernel-only `*LawsTest` covered them). **Four are WORSE than IG22/IG23:** they throw an *uncaught* `ClassCastException`/`IllegalStateException`, not the caught `UnsupportedConstructException`, so they **abort the entire `validate()`/`compute()` run**, not degrade one rule. Two shared root mechanisms: **(R1)** `pathOf` (`Interpreter.kt:1101`, `e as Expr.Field`) assumes every predicate/count operand is a plain field — any starred/group operand crashes; **(R2)** `+` is numeric-only (`evalArith`→`num`) — string operands crash.

- **IG24 — string concatenation `[A] + " " + [B]` (`Concatenation`) crashes (R2).** Two present string operands hit `num(Val.Txt)` → `IllegalStateException`. **Highest real-world impact** — `FullName = FirstName + " " + LastName` is one of the most common computations; it aborts `compute()` the moment the fields are filled. Fix: kind-dispatch `+` (string concat when operands are text, else numeric add), matching the kernel's overloaded `+`. Lock with a compute differential. (Renderer `DslRenderer.java:349`; only `ConcatenationReadTest` today.)
- **IG25 — group-scoped field-fill over a star/group (`GroupScopedFieldFill`) crashes or is silently wrong (R1).** `AtLeastOneFieldFilled(/Order/Items*)` (starred) → `Expr.Star` → `pathOf` `ClassCastException` (crash); `AllFieldsFilled(/Order/ShippingAddress)` (bare group) → treated as ONE field operand → **quiet wrong verdict** (the worst kind). The kernel means "the fields *inside* the group". Fix: expand a starred/group quantifier operand to its member cells before the tally. Lock with a validate differential (both forms). (`quantTally` `Interpreter.kt:512`; only `GroupScopedFieldFillReadTest`.)
- **IG26 — `NumberOfFilledGroups(Group*)` / `RootGroupCount` (starred group count) crashes (R1).** A starred group arg → `Expr.Star` → `pathOf` `ClassCastException`. `NumberOfFilledDiffTest` only feeds *plain* group refs. Fix + a differential over the starred form. (`Interpreter.kt:737`; `RootGroupCountReadTest`/`RootGroupCountLawsTest` are render/kernel-only.)
- **IG27 — entity-valued value list `… In $Group*/x, $Group*/y` (`FieldListEntityListCondition`) has TWO failures.** The `$` parse (IG23) **and**, independently, a starred value ref → `evalValueListQuant` maps `evalExpr` over the values → `Expr.Star` → `error(...)` crash. So fixing IG23's `$` alone will NOT make it work — the value-list star path needs handling too. Lock with a differential over the entity form (`ValueListQuantifierDiffTest` covers only the literal form).
- **IG28 — German render target unparseable (low, likely by-design).** `DslRenderer(ConditionLanguage.DE)` emits `Und`/`FeldAngegeben`/`Heute`/`@Von`; the interpreter parser hard-codes English keywords. The interpreter is EN-only today; decide whether to keep it EN-only (declare out-of-scope, since the differential authors EN and the kernel accepts EN) or make the parser language-parametric. Not a crash on any EN-rendered model.

**The reported IG23 condition hits BOTH IG23 and IG25** (`AtLeastOneFieldFilled(Star Having …)` is a filtered quantifier over a star), so the `$` fix must also handle the starred-quantifier-with-`Having` path. **The durable guard that closes the whole class (so "IG23 can't happen again"): a renderer-vocabulary round-trip differential** — enumerate every construct `DslRenderer` can emit, author each via the DSL, and assert it parses AND agrees with the kernel through `DmInterpreter`; plus teach `InterpreterKernelFuzzTest` to generate stars/groups/`$`/concatenation. Keyed on the RENDERER's output, not the operator inventory — the one net the operator-keyed guards structurally cannot provide.

**The partial-validation FUZZ differential is built** (`adapter/…/perf/PartialValidationFuzzDiffTest`, 2026-06-26): it sweeps random (document, relevant-subset) combinations and asserts the interpreter's `validatePart` matches the kernel's fired-message signatures across the WHOLE partial surface (including the starred aggregates — it surfaced IG20, now fixed as IF42 and un-excluded). It is the standing guard for the relevant-set dimension.

Deferred items are in §2; a fixed gap is deleted from here (git + its `IFn` finding are the record).

Re-open trigger: a kernel upgrade that wires `UNIQUE_VALUE` into `AutogeneratedRulesService` or an `IAutogeneratedRule`. Detail: KERNEL-FINDINGS "multiSelect value uniqueness is DEAD code"; locked by `adapter.laws.MultiSelectUniquenessDiffTest`.
